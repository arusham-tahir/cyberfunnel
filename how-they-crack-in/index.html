How They Crack In...

Not every password gets stolen through a data breach. Sometimes, hackers actively try to guess or force their way into your accounts using a few different methods, each one exploiting a different weakness in how people create and use passwords. Let's go through them one by one.

Click on each (+) sign to reveal each method...
1. Brute Force Attack🔨

This is the simplest method, where a hacker's software tries every possible combination of characters until it finds the right password. A short password like "cat123" might only take a few seconds to crack this way, since the tool simply keeps guessing, letter by letter, number by number, until it stumbles onto the right one. The shorter and simpler the password, the faster this method works.

2. Credential Stuffing📋

Since so many people reuse passwords, hackers take leaked username-password pairs from one breach and try them on dozens of other websites, banking apps, email, social media, hoping you used the same login everywhere. For example, if your Netflix password leaked in a breach and you use that exact same password for your email, a bot could test it there automatically within minutes, without anyone even targeting you personally.

3. Dictionary Attack📖

Instead of trying random characters, this method uses a list of common words, names, and popular passwords, like "password123," "iloveyou," or "qwerty", to guess logins faster than brute force alone. These lists are built from the most commonly used passwords worldwide, so if your password is a real word or a predictable phrase, it's likely already sitting in one of these lists.

4. Phishing🎣

Sometimes hackers don't need to guess at all, they simply trick you into typing your password into a fake login page that looks real. For example, you might get an email that looks exactly like it's from your bank, asking you to "verify your account" through a link. The page looks identical, but the moment you type in your password, it goes straight to the hacker instead.

Each of these methods works differently, but they all rely on one thing: predictable or reused passwords. The harder you make it to guess, the safer you are.
